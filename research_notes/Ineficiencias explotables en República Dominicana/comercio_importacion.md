# Ineficiencias de precio en comercio, importación y reventa en República Dominicana (2025-2026)

Notes compiled 2026-10-07. Scope: legal arbitrage for an individual with technical skills and US$500–20,000 of capital. Every claim carries an inline source. Where a source is an aggregator or commercial site rather than a primary one (Aduanas, DGII, Pro Consumidor), that is flagged.

---

## 1. Import rules for individuals: US$200 de minimis, duties, ITBIS, courier costs, largest price gaps

### Takeaway
The US$200 courier exemption (Ley 277-12, backed by DR-CAFTA and court rulings) is still in force in 2026. The government tried to tax these shipments in the October 2024 fiscal reform, then withdrew the whole bill. Courier freight from Miami costs about US$2.8–4.3/lb. That makes light, valuable goods under US$200 the clearest structural arbitrage. Above US$200, the 18% ITBIS plus any tariff shrinks the gap a lot. For example, an iPhone 17 bought in the US and landed in the DR costs about the same as the low end of local street prices.

### Cited Findings
**De minimis status**
- Internet purchases up to US$200 are tax-exempt under Ley 277-12. DR-CAFTA includes a provision protecting purchases under US$200 from taxation. — [Acento editorial](https://acento.com.do/editorial/una-disposicion-ilegal-y-arbitraria-de-la-direccion-general-de-aduanas-8160613.html); [El Dinero](https://eldinero.com.do/?p=98301)
- A 2014 Tribunal Superior Administrativo ruling ordered Aduanas to stop taxing purchases up to US$200. In 2020 the Tribunal Constitucional rejected an appeal against that ruling. — [El Caribe](https://www.elcaribe.com.do/sin-categoria/tsa-falla-contra-aduanas-litis-por-compras-por-internet/) (summarised in search results)
- Sub-US$200 shipments historically paid only a fee of US$0.25 per kg (Asodec, Oct 2013; this is old data and the current fee was not verified). — [Diario Libre, 2013](https://www.diariolibre.com/economia/compradores-por-internet-se-ahorran-hasta-300-segn-asodec-HMDL405992)
- The October 2024 "Ley de Modernización Fiscal" proposed removing the exemption. It would have applied ITBIS and selectivo while keeping tariffs at zero, with projected revenue of RD$4,173.9 million. The rationale was "unfair competition" with local merchants. — [Diario Libre, 7 Oct 2024](https://www.diariolibre.com/economia/macroeconomia/2024/10/07/reforma-tributaria-sobre-impuestos-en-compras-por-internet/2873009)
- President Abinader withdrew the whole reform on 18 Oct 2024, after the courier association and the public objected. — [El Día](https://eldia.com.do/el-retiro-del-proyecto-de-modernizacion-fiscal/); [Diario Libre, 19 Oct 2024](https://www.diariolibre.com/politica/general/2024/10/19/el-gobierno-retirara-la-reforma-fiscal-del-congreso-nacional/2885356); [Diario Libre – Asodec asks for revision](https://diariolibre.com/actualidad/nacional/2024/10/09/asociacion-de-courier-solicita-revision-urgente-de-la-reforma-fiscal/2875801)
- A 2025-2026 commercial guide still describes purchases of US$200 or less as exempt from customs taxes in the DR. — [Encárguelo blog](https://encarguelo.com/blog/cuanto-es-lo-maximo-que-puedo-pasar-por-la-aduana-sin-pagar-sobrecostos/) (aggregator)

**Duties above US$200** (aggregator source, treat as indicative; the exact rate depends on the HS code)
- Indicative tariffs: phones 8%, laptops 3%, general electronics 8%, cosmetics 14%, clothing 20%, footwear 20%, tools 5%. ITBIS is 18%, charged in cascade on CIF + tariff + selectivo. DR-CAFTA preferential rates apply to US-origin goods that have a certificate of origin. — [importrd.do/aranceles (2026)](https://importrd.do/aranceles) (unofficial aggregator; its vehicle selectivo figures conflict with other sources, see Section 3)
- Courier-specific explainer on taxes for purchases over US$200. — [CPS-DOM](https://cps-dom.com/impuestos-por-compras-mayores-a-200-dolares-republica-dominicana/)

**Courier rates, Miami to DR (2025-2026 published rate pages)**
- Domex: Priority US$4.17/lb (0–5 lb), US$3.76/lb (5–25 lb); Air Cargo US$3.47/lb (+25 lb). — [Domex tarifas Miami](https://www.domex.do/en/tarifas/Miami)
- Jet Pack: Priority US$2.00–4.00/lb depending on branch; Priority Corporate US$3.23/lb; Air Cargo US$2.82/lb (+30 lb). — [Jet Pack tarifario](https://jetpack.com.do/tarifario/)
- Hola! Courier: US$4.30/lb (1–2.99 lb), US$4.15 (3–9.99 lb), US$4.00 (10–19.99 lb), cheaper for heavier packages. — [Hola Courier](https://holacourier.do/)
- ExtremePaq: RD$185/lb air freight. — [ExtremePaq](https://www.extremepaq.com.do/)
- BM Cargo charges about RD$100 for home delivery in certain zones. It has 37 branches; Aeropaq has 19. — [El Dinero – courier competition](https://eldinero.com.do/83159/los-courier-en-republica-dominicana-luchan-en-precios-velocidad-y-servicio/); [BM Cargo](https://bmcargo.com/en/courier)
- No 2025-2026 published per-pound rates were found for EPS, Aeropaq or Caribe Pack (see Gaps).

**Market size and context**
- DR e-commerce was above US$5.2 billion in 2025, growing about 17% a year. Imported packages rose from about 4 million in 2014 to about 14 million by end-2023, and couriers moved more than 31.8 million packages in 2022–2023. — [El Caribe – Amazon/SHEIN/Temu battle](https://www.elcaribe.com.do/panorama/dinero/competencia-amazon-shein-temu-republica-dominicana/); [Revista Mercado](https://revistamercado.do/technology/e-commerce-technology/e-commerce-en-republica-dominicana-cifras-tendencias-y-futuro/)
- Temu's local delivery partner (MIA Cargo SRL) had made more than 3.26 million deliveries in the DR by Oct 2025. — [Diario Libre opinion, 8 Oct 2025](https://www.diariolibre.com/opinion/agora/2025/10/08/los-problemas-con-temu-y-su-agencia-de-envio-en-rd/3270998)
- Couriers employ more than 4,500 people in the DR. — [News in America, 2026](https://newsinamerica.com/pdcc/boletin/2026/empresas-couriers-aportan-mas-de-4500-empleos-en-republica-dominicana/)

**Price-gap example: iPhone 17**
- The iPhone 17 (256 GB) launched at US$799 in the US. In the DR, new sealed 256 GB units sell for RD$56,000–70,000. Altice offers it at RD$40,290 on a postpaid plan, which is a subsidised price. — [Revista Mercado](https://revistamercado.do/technology/devices/a-cuanto-bajara-el-iphone-17-en-rd-tras-la-llegada-del-iphone-18/)

### Inferences
- **Sub-US$200 rule.** Below US$200 the landed cost is roughly US price + US sales tax at the Miami address (often 0–7%) + US$3–4.3/lb freight. There is no ITBIS and no tariff. Local retail carries 18% ITBIS, tariffs, importer and retailer margins and inventory costs. So the structural gap on small items is at least about 18–40%. It is larger for categories with 14–20% tariffs (cosmetics, clothing, footwear), where tariff and ITBIS compound to about 35–42% before retail margin.
- **Best categories under this rule.** High value per pound and price under US$200: supplements, cosmetics and skincare, sneakers and apparel, small electronics accessories, phone parts, hand tools, and auto parts (sensors, filters, brake pads, small modules).
- **Freight per pound kills cheap or heavy goods.** A 5 lb item pays about US$17–21 in freight. Items under US$30 or bulky low-value goods (diapers, formula in bulk) show much smaller gaps after freight.
- **Above US$200, worked example (iPhone 17).** US$799 × ~1.07 FL sales tax ≈ US$855, plus about US$5 freight. Adding 18% ITBIS (and an 8% tariff if no CAFTA origin) gives about US$1,010–1,090 landed. At an exchange rate assumed near RD$63/USD (the rate was not verified in this research), local prices of RD$56,000–70,000 equal about US$890–1,110. **The gap is near zero or negative.** Local resellers likely undercut formal retail through informal channels. Phones are not a strong arbitrage for a compliant reseller.
- **Legality.** The exemption is for personal purchases. Buying in volume to resell and splitting orders to stay under US$200 is legally gray at best and may be treated as undervaluation or fractioning. A compliant reseller should register with DGII (RNC), declare commercial imports, and charge ITBIS. The structural edge for a legal business is then smaller: mostly the inefficiency of local distribution (thin assortment, high retail margins), not tax avoidance.
- **Bot and data angle.** Build a landed-cost calculator: scrape Amazon or Walmart prices, add courier rates by weight, the tariff by HS code and ITBIS, then compare against scraped Corotos, Facebook Marketplace and local store prices to rank SKUs by net margin. The same engine could be sold as a "¿Me conviene comprar afuera?" consumer tool, monetised through courier affiliate deals.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 published per-pound tariffs were found for EPS, Aeropaq or Caribe Pack. The current Aduanas per-kg fee for sub-US$200 parcels was also not verified (the US$0.25/kg figure is from 2013).
- No official current Aduanas text was found on how shipments to the same consignee are aggregated or how commercial-volume courier imports are treated.
- The exact current tariffs by HS code were not confirmed against Aduanas's official arancel. The importrd.do figures are unofficial.
- No systematic dataset of US-vs-DR price gaps by category was found. The ASODEC claim that buyers save "200–300%" dates from 2013 and is unsubstantiated ([Diario Libre](https://www.diariolibre.com/economia/compradores-por-internet-se-ahorran-hasta-300-segn-asodec-HMDL405992)).

## 2. Local resale channels (Corotos, Facebook Marketplace, Instagram, Mercado Libre, Amazon)

### Takeaway
Corotos is the dominant classifieds platform, with more than 1.3 million unique users a month. Amazon, SHEIN and Temu compete aggressively and deliver through couriers. About 7 in 10 adults shop online. No source in this research confirmed a Mercado Libre presence in the DR. Reseller margins were not documented by any reliable source.

### Cited Findings
- Corotos has more than 1.3 million unique monthly users and covers more than 70 categories. Adevinta sold it to local entrepreneurs (Álvaro De Camps, Javier Lara, Sergio Darío Reyes). — [Diario Libre – Corotos ownership change](https://www.diariolibre.com/economia/corotos-cambia-de-propietarios-LI19663594); [Revista Mercado](https://revistamercado.do/technology/e-commerce-technology/e-commerce-en-republica-dominicana-cifras-tendencias-y-futuro/)
- Corotos sells paid "Planes Corotos" for professional sellers. — [CDN](https://cdn.com.do/estilos-de-vida/planes-corotos-las-nuevas-soluciones-de-ventas-para-profesionales/)
- LaPulga.com.do is another classifieds site (traffic data on Ahrefs). — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/websites/lapulga.com.do)
- The DR has 10.2 million internet users (88.6% of the population), and 7 in 10 adults shop online. — [Revista Mercado](https://revistamercado.do/technology/e-commerce-technology/e-commerce-en-republica-dominicana-cifras-tendencias-y-futuro/)
- The US Commercial Service has an e-commerce overview of the DR. — [trade.gov](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/dominican-republic-ecommerce)
- Couriers such as BM Cargo actively market Temu and Amazon purchasing to consumers. — [BM Cargo – Temu](https://www.bmcargo.com/noticias/descubre-temu-con-bmcargo-ahorra-y-disfruta-desde-republica-dominicana)

### Inferences
- **Opportunities.** The channels are fragmented (Corotos, Facebook Marketplace, Instagram, WhatsApp), and listing prices on Corotos are public. That allows:
  - (a) underpriced-listing alerts, i.e. arbitrage inside the DR on used electronics, tools and car parts;
  - (b) price-history analytics;
  - (c) cross-referencing listed prices against US landed cost to find categories where local sellers mark up the most.
- **Competition.** Consumers can now order directly from Temu, SHEIN and Amazon. A reseller adds value mainly through immediate availability, cash or financing, warranty, and categories that are hard to self-import (oversized, batteries/hazmat, items over US$200).

### Gaps
- No reliable data was found on typical reseller margins, Instagram store economics, or Corotos listing volumes by category.
- Mercado Libre's presence and Amazon's direct-delivery coverage to the DR were not confirmed.
- Corotos terms of service on scraping were not checked.

## 3. Used cars: importing from US auctions (Copart/IAAI)

### Takeaway
Importing a used car carries about 55–60% of CIF in taxes: 20% gravamen, 18% ITBIS on CIF + gravamen, 17% primera placa and 0–3% CO2. Cars made in the US with a CAFTA certificate skip the 20% gravamen. Two rules wipe out the typical "Copart arbitrage" for light vehicles: cars older than 5 years are banned (Ley 4-07), and salvage-title vehicles are banned (Ley 63-17, Decreto 671-02). Only clean-title, recent, US-built cars are viable. That is dealer territory, with high capital needs and thin margins.

### Cited Findings
- **Official taxes, all on CIF:** Gravamen 20% (DGA); ITBIS 18% on CIF + gravamen (DGA); Primera placa 17% (DGII); CO2 0–3% (DGII). CO2 bands: <120 g/km 0%, 120–220 g/km 1%, 221–380 g/km 2%, >380 g/km 3%; EVs 0%. — [Ubikarros guide, 7 Jul 2026](https://ubikarros.com/guias/impuestos-importar-vehiculo-republica-dominicana/) (secondary guide; consistent with [Infotrámites RD](https://infotramitesrd.com/impuestos-para-traer-carro-a-rd/) and [CuantoMeCuesta](https://cuantomecuesta.com/rd/importacion-vehiculo-aduanas/))
- **Worked example:** US$30,000 invoice + US$200 freight + US$150 insurance gives CIF US$30,350. Taxes are gravamen US$6,070, ITBIS US$6,555.60, placa US$5,159.50 and CO2 (1%) US$303.50, totalling about **US$18,088 (≈60% of CIF)**. — [Ubikarros](https://ubikarros.com/guias/impuestos-importar-vehiculo-republica-dominicana/)
- **Example with CAFTA origin:** a 2024 Toyota Corolla 1.8L from the USA with CIF US$22,500 pays about US$9,270 in taxes and costs (~41%), for a total outlay of about US$31,770. — [search summary of ubikarros/infotramites sources](https://infotramitesrd.com/impuestos-para-traer-carro-a-rd/) (it was not verified which page this figure came from)
- **DR-CAFTA:** cars *manufactured* in the USA with a valid certificate of origin are exempt from the 20% gravamen. Buying in the US is not enough. — [Ubikarros](https://ubikarros.com/guias/impuestos-importar-vehiculo-republica-dominicana/); [Listín Diario, 1 Apr 2024 – FTAs allow tariff-free vehicles](https://listindiario.com/economia/finanzas/20240401/acuerdos-libre-comercio-permiten-importar-vehiculos-libres-arancel_802230.html)
- **Age limit:** Ley 4-07 modified Ley 147-00/12-01 to prohibit importing used light vehicles older than 5 years and used heavy vehicles older than 15 years. — [Ley 4-07 (DGII)](https://ws945695e.dgii.gov.do/legislacion/leyesTributarias/Documents/Codigo%20Tributario%20y%20Leyes%20que%20lo%20modifican%20y%20complementan/4-07.pdf); [Aduanas guide on the prohibition](https://www.aduanas.gob.do/media/1jijimol/02-08_que_prohibe_import_vehiculos_guia.pdf). Aduanas enforces a rolling model-year cutoff: for example, from 1 July 2019 only 2014 and newer. — [CDN](https://cdn.com.do/destacados/desde-este-1-julio-los-vehiculos-importados-deben-2014-adelante/); [Aduanas press art](https://aduanas.gob.do/media/3qrfvsrr/20190612-arte-de-prensa-año-vehicular.pdf)
- **Salvage ban:** under Ley 63-17 and Decreto 671-02, salvage vehicles (crash, fire, flood) or vehicles not fit to circulate in the country of origin are prohibited. They are seized on arrival. — [Aduanas notice reiterating the salvage ban](https://www.aduanas.gob.do/media/2wposatd/aviso-reiteracion-prohibicion-vehiculos-usados-salvamento.pdf)
- **Enforcement:** Aduanas dismantled a network importing "cut" vehicles and parts for reassembly and retained 166 vehicles. — [El Día](https://eldia.com.do/aduanas-desmantela-red-ensamblaba-vehiculos-cortados-para-venderlos/); [El Caribe](https://www.elcaribe.com.do/sin-categoria/desmantelan-red-que-ensamblaba-vehiculos-cortados/)
- **Valuation:** Aduanas uses reference values by brand, model and year and may adjust invoices declared below them upward. Insurance defaults to 2% of the invoice. — [Ubikarros](https://ubikarros.com/guias/impuestos-importar-vehiculo-republica-dominicana/); the official Aduanas vehicle tax calculator guide (Oct 2023) confirms the CIF inputs (invoice, freight, insurance at 2% default, treaty selection). — [Aduanas calculator guide](https://www.aduanas.gob.do/media/ggslyvln/guia-calculadora-impuestos-veh%C3%ADculos.pdf)
- **Market:** the US is the top origin of imported vehicles (18,514 units in H1 2025). Used vehicles dominate (43,869 units, +1.83%). — [El Día](https://eldia.com.do/ee-uu-lidera-importacion-de-vehiculos-a-rd/). Vehicle imports had "not recovered" by Mar 2026, and vehicle-related tax collections fell in Jan–Feb 2026. — [Diario Libre, 30 Mar 2026](https://diariolibre.com/economia/negocios/2026/03/30/importaciones-de-vehiculos-al-pais-no-logra-recuperarse/3485488); [El Día](https://eldia.com.do/caen-recaudaciones-por-impuestos-vinculados-vehiculos/)
- **Bureaucracy and taxes** are cited as a burden on importers. — [El Dinero](https://eldinero.com.do/199566/burocracia-e-impuestos-pesan-sobre-la-importacion-de-vehiculos/); a CREES think-tank piece on high vehicle taxes — [CREES](https://crees.org.do/?p=18185)
- **Source conflict:** importrd.do lists a vehicle "selectivo" of 16–130% by engine size, and Torreto Motors cites an ISC of 17–20%. These appear to confuse the 17% primera placa with a selectivo. The more detailed, recent guides use 20/18/17/CO2. — [importrd.do](https://importrd.do/aranceles); [Torreto Motors](https://torretomotors.com/impuestos-y-aranceles-aplicables/)

### Inferences
- **Effective tax load.** For a non-CAFTA-origin car (e.g. Japanese-built), total taxes are about 59–61% of CIF. For a US-built car with a certificate, about 39–41%. Add auction buyer fees, inland US transport, ocean freight (RoRo/container), port charges and a customs broker.
- **The classic Copart play is closed.** Buying a damaged car cheaply, repairing it, and reselling is illegal for import into the DR (salvage ban). Clean-title cars from dealer auctions (Manheim/ADESA), or clean-title lots on Copart/IAAI, are the only legal route. They trade near retail, so margins depend on (a) the DR's high local prices for in-demand models and (b) Aduanas reference values versus actual purchase prices.
- **Capital and returns.** One car needs about US$15,000–35,000 all-in, at or beyond the user's capital ceiling. The return per unit is uncertain. No source documented typical dealer margins.
- **Risks.** Valuation adjustments, seizure if the title history is misread (salvage/flood brands), the rolling year cutoff, slow sales, and FX.
- **Bot angle.** Scrape clean-title auction lots, filter by model year (within 5 years), US assembly plant (VIN digit 1 = 1/4/5 for US-built), and title brand. Compute DR landed cost with CO2 bands. Compare with scraped Corotos/SuperCarros asking prices to flag models with the largest spread. Selling this as a service to small dealers ("dealers de patio") is likely lower-risk than taking inventory yourself.

### Gaps
- No reliable per-unit dealer margin data or side-by-side US-vs-DR price comparisons for specific models.
- Exact Aduanas reference-value tables were not obtained.
- No primary source confirmed current ocean freight costs from Florida to DR ports. Commercial pages exist (e.g. [BR Logistics](https://www.brlogistics.net/es/enviar-vehiculo-a-republica-dominicana/)) but were not read.
- The Corolla example figure could not be traced to a specific page.

## 4. Auto parts, machinery, and B2B importing from China

### Takeaway
Very little source material was found. Structurally, parts and tools carry low tariffs (tools about 5%, from an aggregator). The large, ageing used-car fleet, fed by about 44,000 used imports per half-year, sustains demand for parts.

### Cited Findings
- Indicative tariff for tools is 5% (aggregator). — [importrd.do](https://importrd.do/aranceles)
- More than 43,000 used vehicles were imported in H1 2025, mainly from the US and Japan. — [El Día](https://eldia.com.do/ee-uu-lidera-importacion-de-vehiculos-a-rd/); [El Día – US & Japan lead](https://eldia.com.do/estados-unidos-y-japon-lideran-importaciones-vehiculos/)
- Aduanas has acted against networks importing "cut" vehicle parts for illegal reassembly. Legitimate parts imports should be documented carefully. — [El Día](https://eldia.com.do/aduanas-desmantela-red-ensamblaba-vehiculos-cortados-para-venderlos/)

### Inferences
- **Auto parts under US$200** (sensors, filters, brake kits, OEM modules) are a strong niche: high value per pound, demand from a large Japanese and US fleet, and local stores with limited stock and high markups.
- **Tool and data angle.** A VIN-to-part-number lookup combined with a landed-cost comparator (RockAuto/Amazon/eBay vs local stores) could serve mechanics and shops.
- **B2B sourcing from China via Alibaba** for SMEs is plausible: a consolidator or sourcing agent model using sea freight LCL. ITBIS and tariffs apply in full, so the edge comes from supplier selection and distribution efficiency.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on specific DR auto-parts price gaps, LCL freight costs from China to the DR, or SME importing margins. This is a priority area for follow-up research.

## 5. Price dispersion inside the DR (supermarkets, pharmacies, building materials, electronics)

### Takeaway
Pro Consumidor publishes weekly price surveys for Gran Santo Domingo across large and small supermarkets, markets, wholesalers (almacenes) and colmados, as PDFs. Gaps for the same item across channels are large: for example, onions at RD$32/lb at PriceSmart vs RD$79/lb at Bravo. This public but hard-to-use data is a natural basis for a price-comparison app or bot.

### Cited Findings
- Pro Consumidor's weekly "Precios Justos" report surveys food prices in Gran Santo Domingo across supermarkets (large and small), markets, almacenes and colmados, and reports min/max changes. Example from 13 Nov 2025: Leche en polvo Rica 1,500 g RD$855.79 (−2.86% over two weeks); brown eggs, dozen, RD$146.19 (−8.40%); Arroz Selecto Marca Blanca 10 lb RD$333.53. — [Pro Consumidor informe, 13 Nov 2025](https://proconsumidor.gob.do/Descargar/Precios-Justos-2025/Informe-Precios-Justo-Productos-Alimenticio-al-13-de-Noviembre-2025.pdf); other 2025 editions: [30 Oct 2025](https://proconsumidor.gob.do/Descargar/Precios-Justos-2025/Informe-Precio-Justo-Productos-Alimenticio-al-30-de-Octubre-2025.pdf), [3 Apr 2025](https://proconsumidor.gob.do/Descargar/Precios-Justos-2025/Informe-Precio-Justo-Productos-Alimenticios-al-03-de-abril-2025.pdf), [12 Feb 2025](https://proconsumidor.gob.do/Descargar/Precios-Justos-2025/Informe-Precio-Justo-al-12-de-febrero-2025.pdf)
- Same-product supermarket spreads reach RD$47: onions RD$32/lb at PriceSmart vs RD$79/lb at Bravo. Markets show narrower spreads (RD$2–12). Rice costs RD$23–27/lb in markets vs RD$27–32/lb in supermarkets. Potatoes cost RD$26–45 in supermarkets vs RD$21–25 in markets. — [Acento – "Comprar en los mercados versus comprar en los súper"](https://acento.com.do/el-financiero/comprar-en-los-mercados-versus-comprar-en-los-super-8922493.html) (date not verified)
- Colmados sell more expensively than other establishments (2022). — [Diario Libre, 15 Feb 2022](https://www.diariolibre.com/amp/economia/negocios/2022/02/15/colmados-venden-mas-caro-que-otros-establecimientos/1640822)
- Supermarket private labels are a competitive lever. — [El Dinero](https://eldinero.com.do/28189/marcas-propias-sello-y-ventaja-para-los-supermercados-dominicanos/)

### Inferences
- **Data opportunity.** The reports are PDFs with averaged tables, not prices by store. A bot that scrapes online catalogues of the major chains (e.g. Sirena, Nacional, Bravo, PriceSmart, Jumbo where they publish online) and pharmacies (Carol, Los Hidalgos, GBC) could offer per-store basket comparison and deal alerts.
- **Monetisation:** affiliate or ads, B2B data sales to CPG brands, or selling to colmados as a purchasing optimiser (buying from almacenes vs supermarkets).
- **Physical arbitrage** (buying cheaply at PriceSmart or markets to resell to colmados) exists informally already. Margins of 10–30% on produce are implied by the spreads, but logistics and spoilage eat them.
- **Risks:** scraping terms of service, chains blocking bots, low willingness to pay.

### Gaps
- No Pro Consumidor surveys of pharmacy, building-materials or electronics-store prices were found in this pass.
- Whether DR supermarket chains expose scrapeable online catalogues was not verified.
- No existing DR price-comparison apps were identified (competitive landscape unknown).

## 6. Free zones and Haiti border trade (Dajabón)

### Takeaway
The Dajabón binational market is heavily exposed to political shocks. The 2023 border closure over the Haitian canal dispute cost Dajabón traders about RD$100 million a day. Partial reopenings followed, with low traffic. No 2025-2026 data was found. It is a high-risk, relationship-driven channel, poorly suited to an outside individual. Free-zone opportunities for individuals were not documented.

### Cited Findings
- In Sept–Oct 2023 the DR decreed a total border closure (from 15 Sept) over the canal dispute. The binational market reopened with only Dominican participants because Haiti kept its side closed. — [Diario Libre, 13 Oct 2023](https://www.diariolibre.com/mundo/haiti/2023/10/13/deslucido-mercado-binacional-haiti-no-abrio-sus-fronteras/2491603); [Acento](https://acento.com.do/actualidad/mercado-binacional-deslucido-con-decision-de-haiti-de-mantener-cerrada-su-frontera-9256188.html); [El Caribe](https://www.elcaribe.com.do/panorama/pais/otra-vez-fracasa-intento-de-mercado-binacional-con-haiti/)
- The Dajabón merchants' association reported losses of RD$100 million a day during the suspension. Central Cibao egg and poultry producers estimated losses above RD$500 million over 58 days. — [search summary of El Dinero / El Caribe coverage](https://eldinero.com.do/?p=251033)
- Later reports describe Haitians crossing again to buy, but with low traffic at times. — [El Día](https://eldia.com.do/haitianos-logran-cruzar-al-mercado-de-dajabon-para-adquirir-productos/); [El Caribe – baja afluencia](https://www.elcaribe.com.do/panorama/region-norte/mercado-de-dajabon-con-baja-afluencia/); [El Nuevo Diario](https://elnuevodiario.com.do/comerciantes-destacan-dinamismo-de-mercado-en-dajabon-con-entrada-de-haitianos/)

### Inferences
- **Dajabón.** The structural inefficiency is real: Haiti depends on Dominican food, eggs, construction materials and plastics, and prices swing with closures. Exploiting it legally means formal export registration, local presence and tolerance of political risk. It is not a good fit for a remote, tech-oriented individual. A data product tracking border status and price signals for Dominican suppliers is a conceivable niche.
- **Free zones** are designed for export manufacturers with large capital. No individual-scale arbitrage was identified.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 Dajabón market volumes, price data or border status were found in this pass.
- No research on free-zone rules relevant to small e-commerce players (e.g. bonded fulfilment).

## 7. Export side: DR products sold online abroad (cacao, cigars, larimar, amber, coffee, fruit)

### Takeaway
DR exports hit a record of about US$14.65 billion in 2025 (+13.4%). Cigars and tobacco reached US$906 million in Jan–Aug 2025, and cacao beans US$668.9 million. Larimar export volume more than doubled in 2025 (more than 230,000 lb vs 95,480 lb in 2024) after it gained denomination-of-origin certification. Unique, origin-protected goods (larimar, premium cigars, fine cacao) are the strongest candidates for direct-to-consumer online sales abroad. Retail-margin and regulatory data were not gathered.

### Cited Findings
- Exports totalled US$14.65 billion in 2025, +13.4% (+US$1.73 billion), per ProDominicana. — [DR Listings news](https://drlistings.com/news/dominican-republic-exports-soar-to-record-146-billion-in-2025) (secondary); [El Caribe – ProDominicana record six months running](https://www.elcaribe.com.do/panorama/dinero/prodominicana-anuncia-record-en-exportaciones-por-sexto-mes-consecutivo/)
- Tobacco and substitutes: US$906 million in Jan–Aug 2025 (10% of exports, third-largest product). Cacao beans: US$668.9 million (+US$237.2 million). — [News in America, 2025](https://newsinamerica.com/pdcc/boletin/2025/dominicana-logra-record-de-exportaciones-entre-enero-y-agosto/); [El Dinero – tobacco record](https://eldinero.com.do/332505/el-tabaco-dominicano-vive-su-mejor-momento-con-cifras-record-de-exportaciones/)
- The tobacco industry contributes US$1,356 million a year. Buyers include the US, China, Germany and Belgium. — [El Caribe](https://www.elcaribe.com.do/panorama/pais/industria-del-tabaco-aporta-us1356-millones-afirma-raquel-pena/); [Acento](https://acento.com.do/economia/estados-unidos-china-alemania-y-belgica-compran-tabaco-dominicano-9554379.html)
- Larimar exports exceeded 230,000 lb in 2025 (vs 95,480 lb in 2024). The growth is attributed to the international denomination-of-origin certification and new markets (US, Germany, UK, Italy). — [Prensa Latina, 19 Sep 2025](https://www.prensa-latina.cu/2025/09/19/exportaciones-de-larimar-dominicano-superan-230-mil-libras-este-ano/)

### Inferences
- **Larimar.** It is mined only in the DR and is now origin-certified, so it fits Etsy, eBay or own-store sales to the US and EU with documented authenticity. Capital needs are low (US$500–3,000 of inventory). The main risks are fakes (dyed stone or resin) and trust.
- **Premium cigars.** Shipping to US consumers involves heavy US regulation (FDA tobacco rules, PACT Act shipping and tax requirements, and carrier bans on tobacco). B2C online sales by a foreign individual are likely impractical. B2B to US retailers or exporters is the realistic route. This comes from general knowledge, not from sources found here; verify before use.
- **Fine or organic cacao and specialty coffee.** Plausible as micro-lots sold to US/EU craft chocolate makers and roasters. Phytosanitary certificates and FDA food-facility registration are required (not sourced here).
- **Mangos and avocados.** Fresh produce needs USDA/APHIS clearance and cold-chain logistics, which does not suit small capital.
- **Bot angle.** Monitor Etsy and eBay sold prices for larimar, amber and cigar accessories against DR wholesale prices to size margins, and track demand by keyword.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on DR wholesale vs foreign retail prices for larimar, amber, cigars or cacao. Margins are unquantified.
- No sourced detail on export procedures for small shippers (ProDominicana or DGA small-exporter rules, US FDA/PACT requirements).
- No data on amber, coffee or mango/avocado export volumes for 2025.
