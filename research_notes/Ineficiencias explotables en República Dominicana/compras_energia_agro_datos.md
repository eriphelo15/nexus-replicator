# Ineficiencias explotables en República Dominicana: compras públicas, energía, agro y mercados de información poco digitalizados (2025-2026)

Research date: 2026-10-07. Scope: legal opportunities for an individual with technical skills (scraping, data, bots/SaaS) and limited capital. Gambling and anything corrupt are excluded. Source quality varies: DGCP's Memoria 2025 is primary. Installer blogs and press are secondary and are flagged as such.

## 1. Public procurement (DGCP / SECP, Ley 340-06 replaced by Ley 47-25)

### Takeaway
The Dominican public market is large: about RD$202-245 bn a year, 65k processes and roughly 70k contracts in 2025. It is digital and has open data plus an API. Since 24 Jan 2026 the new Ley 47-25 has raised the MIPYME set-aside to 30% (20% general plus 10% women-led), raised advance payments to 30%, and removed bid-security bonds. That cuts the working-capital barrier for small suppliers. The DGCP portal already sends free email alerts, so a paid alert tool would have to sell analytics (competitor and price intelligence, win-probability, regional MIPYME quota tracking) rather than plain notifications. Payment uncertainty is still the main risk.

### Cited Findings
**Market size and activity (2025)**
- 65,105 procurement processes were published on the SECP in 2025, with an estimated value of RD$245,000 million (+16.4% y/y). 21,083 supplier companies took part — [DGCP Memoria Institucional 2025](https://www.dgcp.gob.do/transparencia/documentos/plan_estrategico/291215_Memoria_Institucional_DGCP-2025_signed.pdf)
- Awards totalled RD$202,390.5 million across more than 70,000 contracts. Another passage of the same report gives RD$200,196 million in 72,580 contracts. Both are internal figures from the same report, so treat ~RD$200 bn (≈US$3.2-3.4 bn) as the order of magnitude — [DGCP Memoria 2025](https://www.dgcp.gob.do/transparencia/documentos/plan_estrategico/291215_Memoria_Institucional_DGCP-2025_signed.pdf)
- Public procurement is about 8-10% of GDP and 30-32% of the national budget — [IADB, Compras y contrataciones del Estado en Centroamérica y RD](https://publications.iadb.org/publications/spanish/document/Las-compras-y-contrataciones-del-Estado-en-Centroam%C3%A9rica-y-Rep%C3%BAblica-Dominicana.pdf) (older study; figure cited in search summary)
- Awards by method, Jan-Nov 2025:

  | Method | Amount (RD$ M) | Share of total |
  |---|---|---|
  | Licitación pública nacional | 158,200 | 62.5% |
  | Procesos de excepción | 47,500 | 23.5% (−12.8% y/y) |
  | Comparación de precios | 14,600 | 7.2% |
  | Compras menores | 10,900 | 5.4% |
  | Licitación internacional | 1,190 | 0.6% |

  Emergency exceptions fell 34% — [DGCP Memoria 2025](https://www.dgcp.gob.do/transparencia/documentos/plan_estrategico/291215_Memoria_Institucional_DGCP-2025_signed.pdf)
- Average time to award: about 54 working days for national tenders and 22 for price comparisons. Overall process times run 58-63 days — [DGCP Memoria 2025](https://www.dgcp.gob.do/transparencia/documentos/plan_estrategico/291215_Memoria_Institucional_DGCP-2025_signed.pdf)

**Competition (bidders per process)**
- DGCP says the processes "attracted ~1.3 million offers". The same report also gives 137,925 offers via the transactional portal plus 13,480 physical offers (91% online), which totals about 151,400. The two numbers are inconsistent. 1.3 M probably counts line-item offers, and ~151k probably counts bid submissions — [DGCP Memoria 2025](https://www.dgcp.gob.do/transparencia/documentos/plan_estrategico/291215_Memoria_Institucional_DGCP-2025_signed.pdf)

**MIPYME and women-owned participation**
- RD$75,725.8 M (37.41% of awards) went to MICM-certified MIPYMES and RD$11,765.6 M (5.81%) to women-led MIPYMES. Large firms took RD$28,800 M (14.23%), individuals RD$1,982 M (0.97%) and unclassified firms RD$83,116 M (41.08%) — [DGCP Memoria 2025](https://www.dgcp.gob.do/transparencia/documentos/plan_estrategico/291215_Memoria_Institucional_DGCP-2025_signed.pdf)
- 10,230 processes were exclusive to MIPYMES or women-led MIPYMES (+11.7% y/y). MIPYMES made up 45% of participants, and 2,760 women-led MIPYMES took part (+21%) — [DGCP Memoria 2025](https://www.dgcp.gob.do/transparencia/documentos/plan_estrategico/291215_Memoria_Institucional_DGCP-2025_signed.pdf)
- Under the old Ley 488-08 / 340-06 regime, 20% of procurement budgets were reserved for MIPYMES, and 5% of that for women-led ones — [DGCP Memoria 2025 via search](https://www.dgcp.gob.do/transparencia/documentos/plan_estrategico/291215_Memoria_Institucional_DGCP-2025_signed.pdf)

**Ley 47-25 (promulgated 28 Jul 2025, in force 24 Jan 2026, replaces 340-06)**
- At least 30% of contracted budgets must go to MIPYME-exclusive procedures: 20% general and 10% women-led, distributed quarterly. Suppliers may be required to be domiciled in the municipality, province or region of supply. Advance payment rises from 20% to 30% at signing without an invoice. Bid-security guarantees (seriedad de oferta) are eliminated, the types of bonds that can be required are restricted, and firms may bid with tax arrears of up to six months. Collusion carries 2-5 years in prison, and corporate fines run from 500 to 5,000 minimum salaries. DGCP must issue rules on the registry, the e-system and framework agreements — [Jiménez Peña Abogados](https://jpadvisors.do/2025/07/30/novedades-de-la-ley-num-47-25-sobre-contrataciones-publicas-promulgada-en-fecha-28-de-julio-de-2025/); [Ley 47-25 text (PHLaw)](https://phlaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Ley-No.-47-25-sobre-Contrataciones-Pu%CC%81blicas-.pdf)
- The law widens scope to the Legislature, the Judiciary and constitutional bodies, and adds lower-value purchase mechanisms with stronger traceability — [OBCP Dominicana](https://dominicana.obcp.es/opiniones/rediseno-de-la-contratacion-publica-dominicana-que-cambia-con-la-nueva-ley-de-compras-47); [El Nuevo Diario](https://elnuevodiario.com.do/compras-y-contrataciones-publicas-en-la-republica-dominicana/)
- A 2026 decree, Decreto 672-26, changes how purchases from MIPYMES work (headline only; details not verified) — [Hoy](https://hoy.com.do/economia/decreto-672-26-diferente-compras-mipymes_1104245.html)

**Supplier registry (RPE)**
- The RPE has about 131,480 records. 80,811 (61.46%) are active, 37,422 (28.46%) are out of date and 12,369 are cancelled or inactive. 751 suppliers were suspended by the regulator, and 689 were suspended during 2025. About 80,600 active suppliers include 11,181 MICM-certified MIPYMES and 4,174 women-led MIPYMES. 7,306 suppliers were new in 2025. Registration is online — [DGCP Memoria 2025](https://www.dgcp.gob.do/transparencia/documentos/plan_estrategico/291215_Memoria_Institucional_DGCP-2025_signed.pdf)

**Payment risk**
- Late payments, red tape, advance tax demands and extra paperwork are among the main obstacles keeping Mipymes out of tenders. Suppliers ask for a certain payment date — [El Día, "Persisten retrasos en pagos del Estado a Mipymes"](https://eldia.com.do/persisten-retrasos-en-pagos-del-estado-a-mipymes/); [El Dinero, del papel a la práctica Ley 47-25](https://eldinero.com.do/341338/del-papel-a-la-practica-el-desafio-de-la-nueva-ley-de-contrataciones-publicas/)

**Open data, API and existing alerting**
- DGCP runs datosabiertos.dgcp.gob.do. In 2025 it logged more than 580,000 queries but only about 3,200 dataset downloads. It also offers a public "API DGCP" for automated access to procurement data. The SICP has modules including DataCompras RD, Planifícate RD, GeoCompras, Data Mercado RD, Mipymes and Compras Verdes, plus an internal alert system for irregularities (SAPR) — [DGCP Memoria 2025](https://www.dgcp.gob.do/transparencia/documentos/plan_estrategico/291215_Memoria_Institucional_DGCP-2025_signed.pdf)
- Suppliers can already set subscription alerts on the transactional portal and receive daily alert emails, filtered by UNSPSC category and geography — [DGCP Portal Transaccional docs](https://www.dgcp.gob.do/transparencia/documentos/proyectos_y_programas/informe_de_seguimiento_de_los_programas_y_proyectos/Informe%20de%20Avances%20Portal%20Transaccional.pdf); [Manual PT Proveedores](https://www.dgcp.gob.do/wp-content/uploads/page/Manual-PT_Proveedores-del-Estado.pdf)
- RD joined an OGP commitment (DO0057) on a portal for competitive tenders — [Open Government Partnership](https://www.opengovpartnership.org/es/members/dominican-republic/commitments/DO0057/)

### Inferences
- **Bids per process.** Dividing about 151k bid submissions by 65k processes gives roughly 2.3 bids per process. That suggests thin competition in many processes, especially compras menores and regional MIPYME-exclusive lots, where the local-domicile requirement under 47-25 narrows the field further. This is an inference: the true single-bidder share was not found.
- **(a) Bidding as a small supplier.** Removing bid bonds and raising the advance to 30% sharply lowers the capital needed. The best fit for a technical individual is IT services, software, data/BI, website maintenance and equipment supply through price comparisons and compras menores (RD$25.5 bn in 2025 combined). Realistic gross margins on reselling goods are often 10-25%. That is not sourced for RD and is a general benchmark. Requirements are an RPE registration (free and online), MICM MIPYME certification to reach the reserved quota, and tax compliance with DGII/TSS. Capital is mainly working capital for the gap between delivery and payment beyond the 30% advance. The main risks are late payment, documentary disqualification and political or relationship bias.
- **(b) Analytics SaaS.** Plain alerts are commoditized because DGCP's own portal sends them free. The gap is intelligence:
  - historical award prices by UNSPSC code and institution, to help price bids;
  - competitor win rates;
  - how far each institution is meeting its 30% MIPYME and 10% women-led quotas each quarter (a 47-25 obligation that creates a quota-gap signal: institutions behind quota must publish exclusive processes);
  - AI-generated bid document checklists, given how often bids are disqualified on paperwork.

  The low download count (3,200) suggests few professional reusers of the data. The pool of target customers is roughly 80k active suppliers, including about 15k certified MIPYMES. Pricing at about US$15-50 per month is plausible but unvalidated.
- A second product is factoring or payment-tracking for receivables from government contracts. Data tools can show each institution's payment speed. Lending capital, however, is beyond limited capital, so the realistic model is partnering with a fintech.

### Gaps
- The true single-bidder share and average bidders per process were not found. They can be computed directly from the DGCP open data or API. That is a recommended first scraping task and also yields marketing content.
- Average government payment times in days by institution were not found.
- No confirmed Dominican-specific private tender-intelligence competitor was found by name. International aggregators such as licitaciones portals cover RD superficially. Absence in search is not proof of absence.
- Whether DGCP publishes in the OCDS standard specifically was not confirmed. The Memoria mentions open data and an API but not OCDS by name.
- The contents of Decreto 672-26 and the 47-25 implementing regulations (thresholds in RD$) were not verified.

## 2. Energy: blackouts, tariffs and distributed solar

### Takeaway
Blackouts are structural and got worse in 2026: 13-20 interruptions per customer per month at the northern and southern distributors by April 2026. Meanwhile tariffs are frozen and subsidized through FETE, with a subsidy gap of about US$2 bn. A January 2026 distributed-generation regulation removed the 25 kW and 1.5 MW size caps, guarantees approval within 45 days, gives free bidirectional meters, and largely keeps net metering. That makes solar plus storage the clearest demand-driven opportunity: about 21,000 net-metering users and roughly 480-530 MW installed as of late 2025. Installers must obtain SIE certification within 3 years.

### Cited Findings
- **Interruptions per customer per month, Jan → Apr 2026:** Edenorte 13.78 → 19.95, Edesur 12.94 → 17.81, Edeeste 8.8 → 13.25. Peak daily demand hit 76 GWh on 10 Apr 2026 (+4.48% vs Apr 2025). Interruption data from SIE — [Revista Mercado](https://revistamercado.do/actualidad/apagones-en-republica-dominicana-empeoran-con-el-calor-interrupciones-aumentaron-hasta-20-veces-al-mes-en-2026/)
- **Deficit and subsidy:**
  - In 2025 the system had a generation deficit of more than 600 MW because of delays and problems at plants (Itabo, Los Mina, Siba Energy).
  - The government faces about US$2,000 M in electricity subsidies.
  - Monomial prices reached about 39 US¢/kWh at an Azua plant in Mar-Jul 2025.
  - Sources: [El Dinero](https://eldinero.com.do/337644/sector-electrico-y-la-crisis-de-nunca-acabar/); [Listín Diario](https://listindiario.com/la-republica/20250820/marranzini-sobre-apagones-partir-finales-ano-problema-estara-subsanado_871015.html); [Diario Libre](https://www.diariolibre.com/amp/opinion/frente-al-statu-quo/2025/09/14/por-que-volvieron-los-apagones/3244845)
- There was a nationwide blackout in November 2025 (falla en San Pedro de Macorís) — [N Digital](https://n.com.do/2025/11/15/apagon-nacional-rd-2025-falla-san-pedro-de-macoris-analisis/)
- **Tariff:** frozen for Jan-Mar 2026 under Resolución SIE-176-2025-TF and subsidized by FETE. The residential BTS-1 tariff has 4 blocks, with higher per-kWh prices at higher consumption — [Bolsillo Práctico](https://bolsillopractico.com/?p=1292); [El Dinero](https://eldinero.com.do/350295/ojala-no-toquen-la-tarifa-electrica/)
- **Net metering scale:** grew from 131 MW (2019) to more than 483 MW by Oct 2025, with more than 21,000 users. MEM puts distributed generation under net metering at 528 MWp — [MEM boletín Oct-2025](https://mem.gob.do/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/10.-Boletin-Informativo-Generacion-y-Gestion-Energia-octubre-2025.pdf); [OPS Energía (installer blog)](https://www.opsenergia.com.do/blog/net-metering-republica-dominicana-vender-energia-solar)
- **Surplus energy compensation (2025),** paid at about 60% of the sale price — [OPS Energía (installer blog)](https://www.opsenergia.com.do/blog/net-metering-republica-dominicana-vender-energia-solar):

  | Distributor | RD$/kWh |
  |---|---|
  | Edesur | 4.44 |
  | Edenorte | 4.35 |
  | Edeeste | 4.53 |
- **Status of net metering is disputed between sources:**
  - An installer blog claims it is being phased out from 2025 to elimination in 2031 — [OPS Energía](https://www.opsenergia.com.do/blog/net-metering-republica-dominicana-vender-energia-solar).
  - A report on the new DG regulation (published 19 Jan 2026) says it keeps net metering. Only low-consumption residential users (<10 kW) in isolated systems pay 25% on injected energy. The 25 kW residential and 1.5 MW commercial caps are replaced by a limit of 12 months' consumption plus 5-10%. Penetration limits are removed. Approval takes at most 45 days (typically 10), with automatic approval if the distributor does not respond. Meters are bidirectional and free. Existing systems have 5 years to transition. Installers need SIE certification within 3 years — [RENSA](https://rensa.net.do/2026/02/11/ahora-si-se-pueden-poner-paneles-solares-nuevo-reglamento-transforma-la-generacion-distribuida/).
  - The 2026 regulation (RENSA) is more recent and should take precedence, but the full text should be checked.
  - A further piece covers net billing ("facturación neta") — [El Dinero](https://eldinero.com.do/276048/facturacion-neta-abre-espacio-al-valor-real-de-la-energia-electrica/)
- **Solar prices in RD (2025-26 market data, April 2026 article)** — [Bolsillo Práctico](https://bolsillopractico.com/hogar-energia/paneles-solares-rd-precio/):
  - Cost per kW: about US$1,100-1,600 for grid-tie systems and about US$1,600-1,800 for hybrid systems with batteries. Lithium storage adds about US$500-800 per kW.
  - System prices: 3-5 kW costs US$3k-7k, 5-10 kW US$7k-12k, 10-25 kW US$12k-40k.
  - Payback: 5-7 years commercial and 7-10 years residential (citing ASOFER). Bills fall 60-85%.
  - Financing: green loans from Banco Popular and BHD, and installer payment plans of up to 10 years.
- **Ley 57-07 incentives:** a tax credit of up to 40% of equipment cost against ISR (income tax) for individuals and companies, and exemption from ITBIS (VAT) and import duties on renewable equipment. Administered by CNE — [Siempre al Día](https://siemprealdia.co/republica-dominicana/impuestos/incentivos-fiscales-para-energias-renovables/); [DGII Consulta técnica 2024 ITBIS energía renovable](https://dgii.gov.do/legislacion/consultas/Consultas%20Tecnicas%202024/Mayo/Consulta%205-ITBIS%20Energ%C3%ADa%20renovable.pdf); [CNE incentivos](https://www.cne.gob.do/wp-content/uploads/2015/06/Incentivos-a-la-Inversion-y-al-uso-de-energias-renovables.pdf)
- Renewables reached about 20% of generation (Jan 2025). The target is 25% in 2025 and 30% in 2030. Solar added 882 GWh in 2025 — [pv magazine Latam](https://www.pv-magazine-latam.com/2025/01/10/republica-dominicana-alcanza-el-20-de-generacion-renovable/); [Sinergya](https://sinergya.com.do/energia-solar-en-rd-de-inexistente-a-cubrir-todo-el-crecimiento-de-la-red/)

### Inferences
- **The inefficiency.** Grid tariffs are subsidized and frozen, while supply is unreliable at 13-20 outages per month. Businesses pay twice: the grid tariff plus diesel generators or inverters. Solar plus storage therefore earns money from three sources: bill savings, avoided diesel costs and avoided downtime.
- **Barriers.** Customers lack information about sizing, ROI and installer quality. CNE paperwork is needed to claim the 40% credit. Financing is the hard part for small and medium firms.
- **Opportunities for a technical individual with limited capital (not a full EPC installer):**
  1. **Lead-generation and quoting SaaS.** Use a bill photo plus geolocation to size a system, compute ROI and generate the Ley 57-07 and SIE paperwork, then sell qualified leads to installers. Lead fees, typically about 3-5% of project value, are an unsourced market norm. Capital needed is minimal.
  2. **Outage-tracking bot.** Aggregate the circuit-level schedules ("programas de circuitos") that the EDEs publish with crowdsourced outage reports, then sell B2B dashboards to businesses, insurers and solar sellers.
  3. **Solar maintenance and monitoring subscriptions** for the stock of more than 21k existing systems. This is a recurring-revenue niche, and SIE installer certification will raise the bar for informal installers.
  4. **Small PPA or leasing for SMEs.** This needs capital or a partnership with banks offering green loans.
- **Risks:** tariff reform, since an unfrozen tariff improves ROI while cuts reduce it; regulatory changes to compensation; and currency risk on USD-priced equipment.

### Gaps
- Exact BTS-1 / BTD tariff values in RD$/kWh for 2026 were not retrieved. Get them from SIE's tariff resolution.
- No quantified data on inverter or battery import volumes or sales growth was found.
- No sourced installer net margins for RD were found.
- Whether the 2024-25 fiscal reform proposals cut the 57-07 incentives is unconfirmed. The 2024 "Ley de Modernización Fiscal" was withdrawn, but later changes have not been verified.

## 3. Agriculture and agro-export

### Takeaway
Intermediation between farm and retail is widely acknowledged. INESPRE's whole model of Mercados de Productores and bodegas móviles exists to bypass it. However, no recent numerical farm-gate vs retail spread was found. Exports are concentrated in cacao (about US$285 M in Jan-Aug 2025, mostly organic) and bananas (more than US$300 M, about 80% organic). Organic banana volumes are falling (−22.6%, 2022-2025), which creates both a gap and a risk. A B2B marketplace linking farmers with hotels and restaurants fits the need but is operations-heavy.

### Cited Findings
- **How food prices are formed:** food passes through collectors, transporters, distributors and wholesale markets such as Merca Santo Domingo. Each adds transport, storage, losses and margin. The basic basket costs more than RD$49,000. The article (19 Jul 2026) gives no quantified spreads — [Acento](https://acento.com.do/actualidad/como-se-forma-el-precio-de-los-alimentos-en-republica-dominicana-9721071.html)
- INESPRE buys directly from producers and sells at subsidized prices through Mercados de Productores, bodegas móviles, fairs and "Comer es Primero / Come con INESPRE". It is expanding provincial programs (e.g., San Juan) — [INESPRE Mercado de Productores](https://inespre.gob.do/servicios/mercado-de-productores/); [FoodNewsLatam](https://www.foodnewslatam.com/paises/91-republica-dominicana/16771-inespre-lleva-alimentos-a-precios-justos-a-san-juan-con-nuevo-programa-provincial.html); [Presidencia](https://presidencia.gob.do/noticias/presidente-abinader-dice-extendera-mercado-de-productores-del-inespre-por-todas-las)
- Hacienda publishes a periodic "Precios Agropecuarios: análisis y seguimiento" report (Jul-Sep 2025 edition dated Nov 2025), and ONE publishes a producer price index. These are public datasets for building price-spread tools — [Hacienda](https://www.hacienda.gob.do/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Precios-Agropecuarios-Analisis-y-Seguimiento-Noviembre-2025.pdf); [ONE IPP](https://www.one.gob.do/publicaciones/2025/infografia-indice-de-precios-al-productor-republica-dominicana-enero-junio-2025/)
- Cacao exports reached US$284.69 M in Jan-Aug 2025 and are almost all organic. Banana exports exceed US$300 M, about 80% organic — [Diario Libre](https://www.diariolibre.com/economia/exportaciones-de-cacao-y-banano-organico-rondan-los-us-300-mm-KM9943950); [El Dinero](https://eldinero.com.do/260066/cacao-de-republica-dominicana-atrae-en-el-exterior-por-su-sabor-aroma-y-calidad/)
- Organic banana exports fell from 204,736 t (2022) to 158,510 t (2025), −22.6%. Revenue fell from US$124.29 M to US$101.47 M, −18.4% — [FreshPlaza](https://www.freshplaza.com/europe/article/9868171/the-dominican-republic-s-organic-banana-exports-have-declined-by-46-000-tons-since-2022/)
- Avocado and banana are flagged as high-growth products, with mango, vegetables and organic ginger emerging — [El Dinero](https://eldinero.com.do/85429/aguacate-y-banano-rubros-con-alto-potencial-de-crecimiento/); [Acento](https://acento.com.do/economia/lo-que-el-pais-vende-y-lo-que-necesita-comprar-en-materia-agricola-9632058.html)

### Inferences
- **Where the inefficiency comes from.** Farmers are fragmented, have little access to price information and lack cold chain and logistics. Intermediaries extend credit and transport, so they capture margin legitimately but opaquely.
- **Data-light, low-capital plays:**
  - A WhatsApp price bot that publishes daily wholesale and farm-gate prices from Merca Santo Domingo, INESPRE and Hacienda data to farmers and buyers, monetized through buyers or ads.
  - Procurement software for hotels and restaurants that aggregates their orders and tenders them to producer groups.
- The physical B2B marketplace (owning logistics) needs vehicles and cold storage and carries spoilage risk, so it is not low-capital. The asset-light version is software plus a commission of about 5-10% (unsourced benchmark), with logistics outsourced.
- **Exports.** For a data person, compliance traceability is a niche: organic certification and EU deforestation rules (EUDR) for cacao. That is inferred; EUDR was not researched here.

### Gaps
- No quantified farm-gate vs retail spreads per product for 2025-26 were found. They could be computed from Hacienda's report against ONE and BCRD retail prices.
- Avocado export value for 2025 was not found.
- Sizing of hotel food procurement was not found. Tourism arrivals data were not collected in this pass.
- Existing agri-marketplace competitors in RD were not identified.

## 4. Poorly digitized information markets (registries, embargos, gazette, RNC)

### Takeaway
Company data is split across 30 chambers of commerce, each running its own Registro Mercantil with different interpretations and paper-based processes. A 2026 MICM resolution seeks to unify criteria but keeps paper. Embargo retentivo practice is abused and there is no centralized lookup. This fragmentation creates demand for aggregated due-diligence and KYC data products for banks, lawyers and real estate. Competitors, data licensing terms and legal limits on personal data (Ley 172-13) were not verified.

### Cited Findings
- RD has 30 chambers of commerce, one per province plus the National District, each administering its own Registro Mercantil. Each fills the regulatory gaps from the 2011 law differently. MICM put a resolution to unify criteria out for public consultation (article dated 7 Jul 2026), but it keeps paper processes despite Ley 126-02 on e-commerce and digital signatures. The author ties institutional fragmentation to the sovereign rating sitting two notches below investment grade — [Acento](https://acento.com.do/opinion/por-que-una-resolucion-sobre-el-registro-mercantil-importa-a-quien-evalua-el-riesgo-dominicano-9713916.html); [Acento, oponibilidad del registro mercantil](https://acento.com.do/opinion/oponibilidad-del-registro-mercantil-210764.html)
- An embargo retentivo lets a creditor freeze up to twice the claimed debt held by third parties such as banks. There is no sanction for wrongful seizures, which leads to abuse — [El Dinero, "El abuso de los embargos"](https://eldinero.com.do/55421/el-abuso-de-los-embargos/); [El Nuevo Diario](https://elnuevodiario.com.do/hacia-un-embargo-retentivo-mas-justo/)
- The DGCP RPE supplier registry is in open data (about 80,600 active suppliers with MIPYME status). That allows company enrichment from government data — [DGCP Memoria 2025](https://www.dgcp.gob.do/transparencia/documentos/plan_estrategico/291215_Memoria_Institucional_DGCP-2025_signed.pdf)
- 284,438 firms were classified as MIPYMES in 2025, and only 29,564 (10.4%) were in the RST simplified tax regime. This is an indicator of the size of the formal SME base — [DGII Informe RST 2025](https://dgii.gov.do/estadisticas/Informes-RST/Documents/Informe%20RST%202025.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Product idea.** Build a unified company profile that merges DGII RNC status (DGII publishes a downloadable RNC list), DGCP supplier and award history, and Registro Mercantil data where accessible. Add sanction and suspension lists such as the 751 RPE suspensions and procurement awards. Sell KYC and credit due diligence to banks, cooperatives, lawyers and real estate brokers.
- **Second product.** Monitor the "Gaceta Oficial", court notices and legal-notice newspapers for embargos, auctions (ventas en pública subasta) and bankruptcy filings. Real estate investors and lenders would pay for early auction alerts.
- **Barriers.** Data is fragmented, partly on paper and spread across 30 chambers. There may be terms-of-use limits on scraping, and Ley 172-13 governs credit information and personal data. Credit bureaus such as TransUnion and DataCrédito are likely incumbents.
- **Legal risk.** Aggregating company data (legal entities) is lower-risk than aggregating personal data. A lawyer should confirm before selling data about individuals.

### Gaps
- Whether the Cámaras' Registro Mercantil has an API or bulk access, and its pricing, was not verified.
- Existing Dominican legaltech or data-aggregation competitors were not identified by name.
- How the Gaceta Oficial is accessed (PDF, searchable or not) was not verified.
- Ley 172-13 specifics were not retrieved in this pass.

## 5. Incentives, tax regimes and programs for a small tech business

### Takeaway
The most relevant advantages are:
- MIPYME certification from MICM, which unlocks the 30% procurement set-aside under Ley 47-25;
- the RST simplified tax regime;
- Ley 688-16 entrepreneurship financing, up to 70% of project financing and interest-free periods;
- Ley 57-07 renewable credits if operating in solar.

Proindustria (Ley 392-07) and zonas francas (Ley 8-90) favor manufacturing or exporters and are less relevant to an early-stage SaaS run by one person.

### Cited Findings
- **Ley 688-16 (Emprendimiento):** startups can access 70% financing in their first three years if they contribute 10% and fund 20% formally, with interest-free loans for 18 months. It also creates the RD-Emprende network and mandates administrative simplification — [El Dinero](https://eldinero.com.do/33781/ley-de-emprendimiento-impulsara-la-creacion-de-empresas/); [Ley 688-16 text (MICM)](https://www.micm.gob.do/transparencia/images/pdf/transparencia/base-legal-de-la-institucion/resoluciones/Leyes/2016/Ley_no._688_16.pdf)
- **Ley 392-07 (Proindustria):** qualified industries can defer ITBIS (paying 1% in advance), get ITBIS exemption on capital goods and technology, and hire foreign technical staff without income tax withholding. It targets industry, not services — [DGII Norma 04-08](https://ws945695e.dgii.gov.do/legislacion/normasGenerales/Documents/NG%20sobre%20Leyes%20de%20Incentivos/norma04-08.pdf); [Diario Libre](https://www.diariolibre.com/economia/diecisis-empresas-se-beneficiarn-de-ley-392-07-EXDL1170051)
- **Zonas francas (Ley 8-90):** benefits apply to export operations — [PHLaw](https://phlaw.com/es/post/zonas-francas-dominicanas-ventajas-de-sus-operaciones-bajo-la-ley-no-8-90/); [PwC regímenes de beneficios fiscales](https://www.pwc.com/ia/es/Tax-legal-news/notas-tecnicas/Regimenes-de-beneficios-fiscales-en-la-Republica-Dominicana.html)
- **RST:** a simplified regime with income caps. RD$8.7 M is cited for agricultural entities, adjusted for inflation. Only 10.4% of MIPYMES use it — [DGII Informe RST 2025](https://dgii.gov.do/estadisticas/Informes-RST/Documents/Informe%20RST%202025.pdf); [Formalízate brochure](https://formalizate.gob.do/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/BrochureRST.pdf)
- **Ley 57-07:** a 40% ISR credit plus ITBIS and duty exemption on renewable equipment (see section 2) — [Siempre al Día](https://siemprealdia.co/republica-dominicana/impuestos/incentivos-fiscales-para-energias-renovables/)

### Inferences
- **Recommended stack:** incorporate an SRL, get MICM MIPYME certification and register in the RPE. That gives access to the reserved procurement quota as a supplier, and it also makes you a credible vendor of procurement analytics to other suppliers. Under 47-25 the quota rises to 30%, and the 10% women-led tranche helps if a co-founder qualifies.
- Software exports to foreign clients may fit a zona franca de servicios, but the setup cost and minimums likely exceed limited capital at the start.

### Gaps
- The exact 2025-26 income ceiling of the RST for service businesses was not retrieved.
- No current 2025-26 startup-specific law (beyond 688-16) or tech-specific tax incentive was found.
- Qualifying criteria and costs for zona franca de servicios for small software firms were not retrieved.
- Grants such as FONDOCyT, Banca Solidaria and the MICM Mipymes digital programs were not researched in this pass.
